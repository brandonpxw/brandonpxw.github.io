Brandon Poon

Software Engineering Student, Singapore Institute of Technology

I’m a Year 1 Software Engineering student at the Singapore Institute of Technology, with a Diploma in Infocomm & Security from Nanyang Polytechnic.

I first got into tech when my mom bought me an iPad back in kindergarten. I played a lot of Minecraft Pocket Edition and I remember being really curious about how the game worked, especially how the game knew how water flows. I still play Minecraft today, along with a bunch of other games.

When I was deciding which diploma to take, my mom told me to go for ICT, not just because she works in the tech field, but also because she saw how bright the future of IT was, and thought it could be bright for me too. Coding was honestly my first big challenge. There were definitely sleepless nights staring at red underlined code wondering what I did wrong, and those days when my project ran perfectly 10 times in a row and then stopped working on presentation day. But with the help of W3Schools and Stack Overflow, I got through it and graduated from Nanyang Polytechnic with a Diploma in Infocomm & Security. Looking back, I realise it was all part of the coding experience.

While I was in poly, I was also part of the Students’ Union EXCO, where I helped run events like NYP Open House and Orientation. I loved running around, talking to people and solving problems on the spot, and it made me realise how much I enjoy helping people, especially when I can make a real difference for them.

After the grind of polytechnic, I went to National Service. NS taught me how to push my limits even when I’m tired or overwhelmed. It also taught me to work with people from all kinds of backgrounds and to stay mentally strong when things get tough. One of my NS friends once said I never seemed to run out of energy, because when everyone was tired and wanted to give up, I would somehow find energy from nowhere and carry on with the mission.

After NS, I started studying Software Engineering at SIT because I really enjoy building things from the ground up. I like watching my projects go from barebones to packed with features to a full running app.

These days, AI is the first place I go whenever I want to learn something new, and I spend a lot of my free time building random side projects. I’m still figuring out exactly where I want to go, but I know what I want my work to do: make a positive difference, even if it’s just for one person.
